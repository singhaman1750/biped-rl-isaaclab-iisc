# Isaac Lab Diagnostics, the Non-Finite Foot Velocity of the Quadruped Co-optimisation

This document records the investigation of the crash of run `quadruped_copt/2026-10-23_08-30-31`, the quadruped co-optimisation task under the estimator architecture `CoptMorALActorCritic`, which ended at learning iteration 334 with `RuntimeError: normal expects all elements of std >= 0.0`. It identifies the quantity that became non-finite, traces how one non-finite reading destroyed the policy, follows the reading back through the Isaac Lab source to the point where it leaves PhysX, and describes the diagnostic written to separate the remaining explanations, with its code explained and its results. Every claim about this repository carries a `file:line` citation, and every claim about Isaac Lab cites `/ws/IsaacLab/source/isaaclab/isaaclab/`, abbreviated below as `isaaclab/`.

## 1. The observation that became non-finite

The terminal output of the run reported NaN at fixed indices of three observation groups. Their positions identify the term once the layout of each group is known.

`privilegedDynamicsObs` concatenates, without history, the joint torque (12 wide), the joint acceleration (12), the foot contact force (4 feet by 3 components, 12) and `feet_lin_vel` (4 feet by 3 components, 12), in that order (`environments/environments/tasks/locomotion/cfg/quadruped/base_env_cfg.py:479-497`). The reported indices 36 to 47 are therefore exactly `feet_lin_vel`, every component of every foot.

`critic` carries a history of 10 frames flattened per term (`base_env_cfg.py:603-608`). Isaac Lab flattens each term's history buffer on its own and concatenates the terms afterwards (`isaaclab/managers/observation_manager.py:423`, `:433`), and the buffer orders a term's frames oldest first (`isaaclab/utils/buffers/circular_buffer.py:80-90`). The terms ahead of `feet_lin_vel` are 3, 3, 3, 12, 12, 12, 3, 12 and 12 wide, 72 channels per frame and 720 over ten frames (`base_env_cfg.py:504-550`), so the reported indices 720 to 839 are the ten frames of `feet_lin_vel`.

The actor input reported NaN at 480 to 695 only in the final block. The policy group is 48 channels per frame over ten frames, 480 in all, and the estimator output `encoderOut` that follows it is 216 wide, so that block is the estimator's output and not an observation.

The NaN appears once per environment, at a reset. After a reset the history buffer copies the first frame it receives into every slot (`isaaclab/utils/buffers/circular_buffer.py:136-139`). The log shows one step on which the current frame and all ten history frames are NaN together, and on every later step the NaN region of the critic shrinks by one frame of 12 channels from its newest end while the current frame stays finite. A single non-finite reading taken on the step of a reset, copied into the whole history and then aged out over ten steps, produces exactly this pattern, and a simulation left in a non-finite state does not.

## 2. How one reading destroyed the policy

The value of the affected environment was NaN on that step, the log printing `NaN in value`. PPO forms returns and advantages from the stored values, so the value loss of the next update was NaN, and a single optimiser step on a NaN loss makes every weight it updates NaN. `CoptPPO` trains the actor, the critic and the estimator under one optimiser, so the estimator output became NaN everywhere, the actor's mean and standard deviation followed, and the sample in `CoptMorALActorCritic.act` raised (`co_optimisation/co_optimisation/modules/copt_actor_critic.py:177`). The plain `ActorCritic` of the single design tasks carries no NaN report, so a NaN there would have crashed the same way without the preceding printout.

## 3. Where the reading leaves PhysX

`feet_lin_vel` returns `asset.data.body_lin_vel_w` for the foot bodies (`environments/environments/tasks/locomotion/mdp/observations.py:198-204`). That property is an alias of the centre of mass velocity (`isaaclab/assets/articulation/articulation_data.py:1071-1073`), which is the buffer returned by PhysX's `get_link_velocities()`, read whenever its timestamp is stale and with no arithmetic applied (`articulation_data.py:650-654`).

The environment step resets the terminated environments and then computes the observations without a physics step in between (`isaaclab/envs/manager_based_rl_env.py:215-238`). The reset events write the root pose, the root velocity and the joint state. Each of these writes stores the written value in Isaac Lab's own buffer before passing it to PhysX (`isaaclab/assets/articulation/articulation.py:427`, `:515`, `:604`, `:642`), so the root velocity and the joint state that the observations read after a reset are Isaac Lab's copies of what was just written. The joint position write also invalidates the cached body velocities (`articulation.py:605-614`), so the post-reset observation reads `get_link_velocities()` afresh, for every environment, from a PhysX state whose root and joints have been set but which has not been stepped. Body poses are guarded against this, their property running `update_articulations_kinematic()` before the read (`articulation_data.py:590-594`), but body velocities are not.

`feet_lin_vel` is therefore the one observation of the quadruped co-optimisation task whose value after a reset comes from PhysX rather than from Isaac Lab's record of the reset, which agrees with it being the only term ever reported non-finite. The joint acceleration is a finite difference Isaac Lab zeroes on the write (`articulation.py:644`), the torque comes from the actuator models, and the contact forces come from the sensor.

## 4. What the logs show about stability

The TensorBoard records establish that the simulation of this robot occasionally produces enormous but finite readings in single design training as well. Run `quadruped_flat/2026-10-21_05-03-23`, the plain task on rough terrain without the `leg_contact` termination, logged one isolated point at iteration 6124 in its only event file, `events.out.tfevents.1792559306.auk6000.2480054.0`, with `Episode_Reward/pen_ang_vel_xy` at -1.53e16, `Episode_Reward/pen_joint_accel` at -2.12e13 and `Train/mean_reward` at -2.3e17, its neighbours at iterations 6122 to 6126 lying between -0.040 and -0.047, -0.161 and -0.185, and 249 and 254. `Loss/value_function` read 1.05 at iteration 6123, infinity at 6124, 1.19e12 at 6125 and 0.995 at 6126. The reward spike is a single point of 7592 per tag, and TensorBoard's interface keeps by default a reservoir sample of 1000 points per scalar tag, so that point is usually not drawn, and where it is drawn the default of ignoring outliers in chart scaling and the smoothing hide it. The value loss spike spans two points and is the more likely to survive the sampling. Launching TensorBoard with `--samples_per_plugin scalars=0` keeps every point. The run survived because the readings were finite.

Run `quadruped_copt/2026-10-22_11-03-57` reached a value loss of 3.3e6 at its first design swap and ended at 5.7e11, and `quadruped_copt/2026-10-08_09-22-09` ended at 5.6e10. The first of these divergences is recorded in [quadruped.md](quadruped.md) section 15. The crashed run itself logged no outlier in any penalty term up to iteration 333, its value loss lying between 0.9 and 2.5 over its last six iterations, so the NaN arose without warning within the iteration that crashed.

The crash preceded the first design swap at iteration 400 and the first controlled CMA-ES evaluation, so neither the swap nor the evaluation of `../plans/copt_improvement.md` took part, and the smoothed tracking width gate of that plan had not yet reached its starting step of 500 iterations. The kit log of the run, `/isaac-sim/kit/logs/Kit/Isaac-Sim/5.1/kit_20261023_083009.log` in `isaac-lab-base-shreyas-gpu0`, carries no PhysX error and no buffer overflow warning. The generation zero documents of the run give every design the donor's foot inertial, 0.02 kg with a diagonal inertia of 4e-6, so no design brings a degenerate foot into the scene.

## 5. The diagnostic

### 5.1 What it tests

Two questions remain after section 3. The first is whether PhysX returns a non-finite link velocity after a reset at all, and if so whether that is a stale readback of an unstepped state, which a forward kinematics update or the next step repairs, or a broken simulation, which neither repairs. The second is whether it depends on the population of distinct designs that only the co-optimisation scene holds, the scene using one USD per design with `replicate_physics=False` (`co_optimisation/co_optimisation/utils/respawn.py:107-119`) where the plain task replicates one asset.

The diagnostic answers both without training. It steps a scene under Gaussian actions, which make the robots fall and reset far more often than a learning policy, checks every link velocity after every step, and records the circumstances of every non-finite reading. It runs the co-optimisation task with its generation zero population as training builds it, and the plain rough task with its single asset as the control, at the same 6400 environments.

### 5.2 The code

The script is `scripts/analysis/nan_reset_diagnostic.py`.

`build_population` reproduces what `CoptOnPolicyRunner.learn` does before its first rollout. It constructs the `GrowingDesignDistCMAESDesignGenerator` with the parameter ranges, seed, late start and population sizes of the `copt` branch of `scripts/rsl_rl/train.py`, constructs a `CoptOnPolicyRunner` with the same `copt` block, and calls `_reload_morphology` under `torch.inference_mode`, which samples generation zero, converts each design to USD, respawns the scene with one USD per design, measures and publishes each design's standing height with a reset, and writes the actuator parameters (`co_optimisation/co_optimisation/runners/copt_on_policy_runner.py:593-615`). The scene is then the one training stepped. The function is skipped for any task whose identifier lacks `Copt`.

The main loop draws actions from a normal distribution of standard deviation `--action_std` and steps the wrapped environment. After each step it reads `robot.data.body_lin_vel_w`, the cached value the observation manager has just read, and marks every environment with any non-finite component in any body. It counts the resets, the non-finite environments, how many of them were reset on that step, the non-finite readings per body, and the observation groups that came out non-finite.

`snapshot` records the first `--max_events` non-finite environments in full. For each it stores whether the environment was reset on that step and its episode length, its design index `env_id % num_designs`, its terrain level and type, the mask of bodies affected, every body's linear and angular velocity and position, the root state, the joint positions, velocities and applied torques, and the foot contact forces. It then takes three further readings that separate the explanations of section 5.1. `fresh_read_finite` reads `root_physx_view.get_link_velocities()` again without any intervening call, which tells whether the PhysX buffer itself holds the non-finite value or whether the cache does. `after_kinematic_finite` reads it after `physics_sim_view.update_articulations_kinematic()`, the forward kinematics that `sim.forward` runs (`isaaclab/sim/simulation_context.py:533-539`) and that the pose properties run, which tells whether an update of the articulation's derived state repairs the reading. The main loop adds `next_step_finite` one step later, which tells whether a physics step repairs it.

At the end the script writes `summary.txt` and `events.pt` to `--out` and prints the summary.

### 5.3 Reading the outcome

| Outcome | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Non-finite readings only on reset steps, repaired by the kinematic update | A stale post-reset readback. PhysX returns link velocities that are not yet defined for an articulation whose state was written without a step, and Isaac Lab reads them because body velocities, unlike body poses, are not guarded by a kinematic update. |
| Non-finite readings only on reset steps, repaired only by the next step | The same readback, but outside what the kinematic update recomputes. |
| Non-finite readings that persist after the next step | A broken simulation, to be traced through the recorded state of the affected design and terrain cell. |
| Readings in the co-optimisation scene and none in the control | The heterogeneous population is a condition of the fault. The recorded design indices then show whether particular designs carry it. |
| Readings in both scenes | The fault belongs to the robot or to Isaac Lab and PhysX, and the co-optimisation scene differs only in rate. |
| No readings in either scene | Gaussian actions do not reach the state the trained policy reached, and the diagnostic must be repeated under the policy of a checkpoint. |

### 5.4 How it was run

Both runs used a launcher that sets `PYTHONPATH` to the mounted `co_optimisation` source and runs from a scratch directory outside the repository, preserved with this document's artefacts.

```bash
bash nan_diag.sh Isaac-Quadruped-Copt-MoRAL-Rough-v0 copt --num_envs 6400 --steps 20000 --copt_num_individuals 80   # isaac-lab-base-shreyas-gpu0
bash nan_diag.sh Isaac-Quadruped-Blind-Rough-v0 single --num_envs 6400 --steps 20000                                # isaac-lab-base-shreyas-gpu1
```

A trial of 300 steps at 640 environments ran both modes to completion before the full runs, with 964 resets in the co-optimisation scene, 1190 in the control and no non-finite reading in either.

## 6a. Penetration between the foot and the leg

Each foot is a sphere of radius 0.022 m on the end of its shank, and the shank's collision box, 0.028 m square and 0.21 m long, is centred 0.105 m down the shank, so it ends at the sphere's centre (`copt_usds/gen_0000/individual_0000.xml` of the crashed run, lines 70-74, and the donor `environments/environments/assets/mjcf/quadruped/quadruped.xml`). The two shapes therefore overlap by the full radius at every pose. Whether that overlap generates a contact depends on whether the physics engine pairs a link with its parent. Isaac Sim documents that adjacent articulation links do not collide by default, while non-adjacent links do when self collision is enabled [10], and the robot is spawned with `enabled_self_collisions=True` (`environments/environments/assets/config/quadruped_identified_cfg.py:79-83`), so the foot can still touch its own thigh, the other legs and the trunk.

`scripts/analysis/self_collision_check.py` measures this offline. It loads each MJCF document with MuJoCo, poses the joints at zero, the nominal pose since the fold lives in the body frames, and at random offsets drawn uniformly within plus or minus 0.2 rad and clamped to the joint limits as `reset_joints_by_offset` draws them (`environments/environments/tasks/locomotion/cfg/quadruped/base_env_cfg.py:1065-1072`), and computes the signed distance between every pair of collision geoms with `mj_geomDistance`. Each pair is labelled adjacent when one body is the other's parent and active otherwise. Over the donor at 2000 reset poses and the 100 generation zero documents of the crashed run at 300 poses each, the only penetrating pairs are adjacent, each foot inside its own shank by 22.00 mm at every pose and each shank inside its own thigh at the knee by 13.76 to 14.50 mm. No active pair penetrates at the nominal pose or at any sampled reset pose, and the geometry is the same in the single design and the co-optimisation scenes.

The static check cannot show whether PhysX honours the adjacency filter for the foot, which hangs from the shank by a fixed joint. The `--pair_contacts` option of `scripts/analysis/nan_reset_diagnostic.py` measures it at runtime. It adds one contact sensor per foot filtered against every shank and every thigh, one sensor per foot because Isaac Lab reports filtered contacts only from one sensor body to many filter bodies (`isaaclab/sensors/contact_sensor/contact_sensor.py:45-58`), reads `force_matrix_w` after every step, and reports for every foot and leg body pair the number of environment steps in contact and the largest force, separating the foot's own leg from the other legs, and records the matrix at every non-finite event. A non-zero force between a foot and its own shank would show the overlap of 22 mm to be in contact, and a force against another leg would show active self collisions occurring in motion.

### 6b. The optional shank to foot gap, added 2026-10-23

To remove the overlap outright, whatever the filter does, the quadruped builder takes an optional `shank_foot_gap` in metres (`design_generator/quadruped/builder.py`, `build_xml` and `_leg_xml`). When it is given, each shank's collision box is shortened from the knee end so that its lower face lies that distance from the foot sphere's surface, the end being placed at `foot_z + sqrt((r + gap)^2 - d_xy^2)` with `d_xy` the lateral distance of the sphere's centre outside the box section, zero for this robot. The inertial block, the visual box, every body frame and the foot position are unchanged, and since the importer takes each body's mass from its explicit inertial rather than from geom density, the dynamics are unchanged as well. When it is None, the default, the document is byte for byte what the builder emitted before, which was verified for the donor and for the four corner designs of thigh and shank lengths 0.16 and 0.27 m. At a gap of 0.001 m MuJoCo measures 1.000 mm between every shank box and its foot in all five, against -22.000 mm before, the shank box of the donor moving from a half length of 0.1065 m to 0.0950 m, the masses and inertias identical and the thigh to shank overlap of 13.757 mm untouched.

The gap reaches the co-optimisation designs through `DesignGeneratorBase.builder_kwargs`, an empty dictionary by default that `_build_xml` passes to `Family.build_xml` and so to every emitted document, the population, the substitute for a refused design and the exported optimum alike (`co_optimisation/co_optimisation/runners/usd_generator.py`). `scripts/rsl_rl/train.py` sets it from `--shank_foot_gap`, unset by default, and records the value in the `copt` block of the dumped `params/copt_agent.yaml`. The donor document `environments/environments/assets/mjcf/quadruped/quadruped.xml` is not regenerated, so the plain quadruped tasks and the startup spawn of the co-optimisation task, which the generation zero population replaces before the first rollout, keep the overlap.

### 6c. Instability as the source, and the solver iterations

The runtime measurement of section 6a, with the filter paths named one per body, found over 960,000 environment steps of the single design scene under Gaussian actions no contact at all between a foot and its own shank or thigh, so PhysX honours the parent to child filter and the overlap of 22 mm was never in contact. It found contacts between a foot and the other legs' links in 361 environment steps, the commonest being a rear foot on the front shank of the same side, `foot_RL` on `shank_FL_Link` in 141 steps at up to 401 N and `foot_RR` on `shank_FR_Link` in 164 at up to 338 N.

The articulation stability guide of the simulator names the conditions under which an articulation diverges, competing hard constraints such as contacts against stiff drives, large mass and inertia ratios, since an impulse applied to a large mass produces very large velocities when propagated to a body of small mass, unintended self collisions between non adjacent links, high joint velocities and accelerations early in training, and drives whose natural frequency times the time step is not well below one [11]. It recommends a generous joint velocity limit of 200 to 300 degrees per second as a guard [11]. Measured against this robot, the drives are safe, a knee stiffness of 50 against an armature of 0.01 kg m^2 and an effective inertia of 0.0237 kg m^2 (`environments/environments/assets/config/quadruped_identified_cfg.py:19-63`) giving a natural frequency of about 38 rad/s and a product with the 0.005 s physics step of about 0.19. The foot is the lightest body, 0.02 kg against a robot of about 15.8 kg, and it is the body that strikes the other legs at up to 400 N. The joint velocity the solver enforces is not the actuators' `velocity_limit` of 20 and 30 rad/s, which shapes only the torque speed curve, but `velocity_limit_sim`, which the quadruped configuration does not set, the distinction recorded for the KScale in [KScale.md](KScale.md), so no joint velocity guard of the kind the guide recommends is in force.

A divergence fits the evidence of sections 1 to 4 once the post-reset read is taken into account. A body whose velocity overflows to infinity yields NaN at the first operation that subtracts or scales infinities. An environment that diverges within a step is terminated on that step, the trunk contact force passing its threshold, and is reset, after which the observation reads the link velocities of the unstepped PhysX state, still those of the diverged articulation, while the root velocity and the joint state come from the values the reset wrote (section 3). The clipped terms of the critic, the base velocities and the joint state among them, could not show such a value as NaN in any case, a clip of an infinity returning the bound. The reading would then be NaN for one frame and finite from the next step, which is the pattern of section 1. Gaussian actions had produced no non-finite reading at 163,852 resets of the co-optimisation scene and 210,431 of the control, which is consistent with a divergence that the fast gait of a trained policy reaches and random actions do not, and the single design run's spike at iteration 6124 shows the same robot diverging to finite values of order 1e16 in the reward.

The solver resolves each step's joints, contacts and limits iteratively. Under TGS, the solver Isaac Lab selects by default (`isaaclab/sim/simulation_cfg.py:37`), the position iterations solve the constraints with an integration inside each iteration, so more of them approach the result of a smaller time step and leave smaller residual penetrations and constraint errors to be corrected by large impulses the next step [11]. The velocity iterations follow and correct velocities alone without moving bodies, so they refine friction and restitution and cannot remove penetration. The scene takes for every actor the highest count any actor requests, clamped to the configured range (`isaaclab/sim/simulation_cfg.py:46-83`). The quadruped requests 8 position and 4 velocity iterations (`quadruped_identified_cfg.py:79-83`), and section 8 of [quadruped.md](quadruped.md) records that the configuration before the reset of 2026-09-17 carried 8 velocity iterations, a value not restored since. Too few position iterations is the setting under which a hard strike between a light foot and a shank, or a foot against stiff rough terrain, leaves a residual that the next step corrects violently, and that is the plausible route to a divergence here, but no run has tested a change of either count.

### 6d. The drive integration margin and the limits PhysX holds

The quadruped's actuators are `IdentifiedActuator`, a subclass of Isaac Lab's explicit `DCMotor` (`environments/environments/actuators/actuator_pd.py:18-46`). An explicit model computes the torque in Isaac Lab from the joint state at every physics step and hands PhysX an effort, PhysX's own drive stiffness and damping being set to zero (`isaaclab/assets/articulation/articulation.py:1766-1769`), so the PD law is integrated explicitly by PhysX's semi-implicit Euler step and not implicitly. For a joint of stiffness `k`, damping `c` and effective inertia `I` under a step `dt`, writing `a = k dt^2 / I` and `b = c dt / I`, the update matrix of that scheme has determinant `1 - b` and trace `2 - a - b`, so it is stable exactly when `0 < b < 2` and `a + 2b < 4`, equivalently `(omega_n dt)^2 + 4 zeta (omega_n dt) < 4`, which for light damping reduces to `omega_n dt < 2`. With the gains of `environments/environments/assets/config/quadruped_identified_cfg.py:21-66`, the effective inertias of `joint_control_analysis_quadruped.md` section 2, which include the armature, and the physics step of 0.005 s:

| Joint | k | c | I (kg m^2) | omega_n (rad/s) | omega_n dt | zeta | c dt / I | a + 2b, stable below 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| abduction, crouch | 40 | 1.2 | 0.023674 | 41.10 | 0.206 | 0.617 | 0.253 | 0.549 |
| abduction, extended | 40 | 1.2 | 0.033342 | 34.64 | 0.173 | 0.520 | 0.180 | 0.390 |
| hip | 40 | 1.1 | 0.020572 | 44.10 | 0.220 | 0.606 | 0.267 | 0.583 |
| knee | 50 | 0.9 | 0.012305 | 63.74 | 0.319 | 0.574 | 0.366 | 0.833 |
| knee, armature alone | 50 | 0.9 | 0.010 | 70.71 | 0.354 | 0.636 | 0.450 | 1.025 |

The last row bounds every design, the armature of 0.01 kg m^2 being the floor of the knee's inertia whatever the shank, so the drives are stable with a margin of about four in the criterion and need no change. A live probe of the plain task in `isaac-lab-base-shreyas-gpu1` read the limits PhysX holds. The joint velocity limit of every joint is 5.94e36 rad/s in PhysX and in `joint_vel_limits`, the importer's unbounded value, since `velocity_limit_sim` is unset and resolves to the asset, while the actuators' `velocity_limit` of 30 and 20 rad/s shapes only the torque speed envelope. The joint force limit in PhysX is 1e9, the default `effort_limit_sim` of an explicit actuator (`isaaclab/actuators/actuator_base.py:106-112`, `:174-175`), the torque itself being clipped by the actuator at 23.62 and 35.24 N m. Every link carries `physxRigidBody:maxLinearVelocity` 1000 m/s, `maxAngularVelocity` 1000 degrees per second in Isaac Lab's units (`isaaclab/sim/schemas/schemas_cfg.py:89-93`), and `maxDepenetrationVelocity` 1.0 m/s, the articulation root requests 8 position and 4 velocity iterations with self collision enabled, and the links carry the importer's 16 and 1. The angular cap did not bound the trunk during the divergence of `quadruped_flat/2026-10-21_05-03-23` at iteration 6124, whose `pen_ang_vel_xy` of -1.53e16 implies a far larger rate, so it cannot be relied upon as a guard. No limit in force bounds a joint velocity or a link's linear velocity below an overflow.

## 7. Published reports bearing on the fault

A search of the Isaac Lab documentation, issue tracker and discussions on 2026-10-23 found no report of a non-finite link velocity after a reset, but found the mechanism of section 3 documented and recently fixed upstream.

The Isaac Lab known issues page states that PhysX combines the kinematic and dynamic updates in a single step, so that writing a root or joint state does not by itself run a forward pass, and that reading `body_link_pose_w` triggers a kinematic update while other readings taken between a reset and the next step are not refreshed [1]. The page names no velocity quantity and no non-finite value.

Issue #8662 reports, on the GPU pipeline of PhysX, that articulation child link poses and velocities read after a root or joint write and before the next physics step return the previous episode's values, because the write only marks the kinematics dirty and the child links are brought up to date only by the manager's kinematic update [2]. It records that `ManagerBasedRLEnv.step` resets and computes observations with no physics step or `sim.forward()` between them, that an earlier change had added `write_data_to_sim()` and `sim.forward()` after the reset and a later one removed them, and that on the branch it was filed against `ArticulationData.body_com_vel_w` calls the kinematic update while sensors reading the physics views directly do not [2]. Its offsets are finite, and it mentions no NaN. The proposed fix of PR #8663, a kinematic update before each raw sensor read, was closed unmerged as superseded [3], and PR #8780, merged into `main` on 2026-10-23, resolves pending kinematics lazily on read and leaves release branch backports to separate work [4]. The vendored tree is Isaac Lab 2.3.0 (`/ws/IsaacLab/VERSION`), whose `body_com_vel_w` performs no kinematic update (`isaaclab/assets/articulation/articulation_data.py:650-654`), so the post-reset velocity read of section 3 is the unguarded read those reports describe.

Two older reports concern non-finite values directly. In discussion #89 a maintainer attributes NaN observations on GPU PhysX to bodies overlapping at reset or penetrating too far into each other, and recommends checking the reset placement and comparing against the CPU pipeline [5]. Issue #8121 reports that an articulation of more than 64 links corrupts other environments under the default `gpu_max_num_partitions` [6], which does not apply to this robot's 25 bodies. Issue #8607 reports contact sensor data that is stale immediately after a reset and contact points that come back as NaN on the following step [7], which bears on the foot contact force term but not on `feet_lin_vel`, the contact force channels never having been reported non-finite. The NVIDIA forum carries the same `normal expects all elements of std >= 0.0` crash from NaN observations in an older Isaac Sim, with no identified source [8], and the Isaac Lab training guide attributes NaN observations to unstable simulation, from aggressive actions or resets into invalid states [9].

These reports support the post-reset read as the point of entry and give two candidate remedies, a kinematic update before the velocity read, which `body_link_pose_w` already performs in this tree and which the fresh and kinematic re-reads of section 5.2 test, and an upgrade to the fixed `main`. They do not establish why PhysX would return a NaN rather than a stale finite value, which the diagnostic must still decide.
