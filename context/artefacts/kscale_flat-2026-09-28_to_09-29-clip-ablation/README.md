# Artefacts of the commanded target clip ablation, 2026-09-28 to 2026-09-29

This directory holds the evidence that section 18 of [../../../plans/kscale_gait_shaping.md](../../../plans/kscale_gait_shaping.md) reasons about, being the five training runs its section 18.1 tabulates together with the two launched on 2026-09-30 that carry the clip excess penalty, and the eighteen checkpoint replays of them that `scripts/analysis/compare.py` staged under `IsaacLab/logs/rsl_rl/kscale_flat_eval/`.

It differs from the four directories before it in one respect that governs how its figures should be read. Every quantity here is READ from the dump rather than inferred from it, `scripts/rsl_rl/play.py` having begun on 2026-09-28 to record the commanded target, the raw action behind it, the pre clip torque, the velocity dependent envelope and the sole tilt, which the addition of that date to [/ws/context/gait_metrics.md](../../../../context/gait_metrics.md) describes. The preceding directories recovered the commanded target by inverting the control law, which is exact only on an unsaturated sample and which reached 11.75 per cent of one run's ankle roll samples, so every figure those directories quote for a saturated joint is a bound where these are values.

Attribution is by construction. Every figure is computed from `kscale_flat_eval/<experiment>_<checkpoint>/data/42/dump.npy`, whose directory name carries the experiment and the checkpoint that produced it, and every replay was configured from the copy of its run's own `params/` that `compare.py:290` places beside the checkpoint before launching.

## A correction retained rather than removed, 2026-09-30

`clipab.py` reconstructs the unclipped target as the raw action times the scale plus the action term's offset, and the offset must be RECOVERED per environment rather than taken from the dump. `default_joint_pos` is environment zero's row alone while `randomize_joint_default_pos` adds up to 0.05 rad per environment at reset, so an excess computed against the dumped row is never exactly zero even on a run carrying no clip at all, and the first pass reported every joint of every run as clipped on 100 per cent of samples.

The first repair, a median over time of the recorded target minus the scaled raw action, is correct on a run the clip does not bind and wrong on run E, where the hip roll clip binds on 91.8 per cent of samples so that the median is the clip bound rather than the offset. It recovered a figure 3.1 rad from the dumped row where the randomisation can account for 0.048. The second and standing repair restricts the median to the samples the clip did NOT bind, and it recovers every run to within 0.04809 rad of the dumped row, which is the randomisation exactly. The weakest support is the ankle roll of run E at 6.04 per cent of samples, which is several thousand and adequate for a median.

The episode is recorded because both figures were plausible and neither announced itself, and because a later reader recomputing the clip excess without the restriction will reproduce the second fault rather than the finding.

## Two programs added on 2026-09-30, and what each was written to settle

`headinggate.py` was written because arm 15 of the plan proposes closing the airborne gate on `pen_feet_heading` on an argument drawn from runs A, B and C, and no measurement of that argument existed against run E, which is the baseline the arm will run upon. It reports three things and each moved a parameter. The toe-in by phase, which shows the stance figure risen by a factor of 2.42 from run A to run E while the swing figure fell, closing the ratio the gate was justified by from between four and eighteen down to 1.92. The term decomposed into its common and differential modes with the gate open and closed, which shows the ungated common mode carrying 1.3 per cent of the term on run E and the gate ENLARGING it by a factor of 25.9 rather than removing a price, since restricting the circular mean to the airborne feet makes it the swing foot's own error. And every contact interval traced from its touchdown sample to the last before release, which shows the heading planted and held on run E and GROWING by 3.642 to 9.632 degrees within the interval on runs A through D, so that the gate's safety is a property of the baseline rather than of the term.

`excess_kernel.py` was written to check the absolute clip excess that section 18.4 of the plan quotes, that section having derived its figure without stating a measurement. The figure is confirmed exactly at 7.3391 radians against the 7.345 the section implies, and the per joint breakdown it adds is what section 21.12 uses to correct the section's calibration, the two hip rolls carrying 14.315 and 14.332 radians squared of the quadratic excess against the two ankle rolls' 0.863 and 0.864, so the ankle roll pair holds 5.7 per cent of the quadratic excess and 20.4 per cent of the absolute one and was wrongly omitted from a calibration whose own sentence names four joints. It recovers the per environment offset by the same restriction to non binding samples that the correction below describes, and it is the second program in this directory to depend upon it.

## Two further programs added on 2026-10-01, for arm 14

`fixedjoint_probe.py` was written to settle one question, whether a URDF joint declared `type="fixed"` keeps its child link as a body of the articulation and whether it leaves `joint_names`. Every draft of the hip yaw fixed arm before that date assumed the child is merged into its parent, which would have taken the articulation to eleven bodies and required `_BODY_PARTNERS` at `mdp/symmetry/kscale.py:80` to be rebuilt. The merge is conditional on `merge_fixed_joints`, which `kscale_identified_cfg.py:279` sets to False, and the SD_BRS1 answers the question without a simulator because it already declares both of its hip yaws fixed under the same setting. Its dump reports ten `joint_names` with no hip yaw among them and a `robot_mass` thirteen columns wide, so the degree of freedom goes and the body stays. The arm shrank accordingly and the symmetry module needs no change at all.

`validate_arm14.py` is the pre launch check that follows from it, and it differs from `validate_arm6.py` in one respect worth stating. It validates a PLAN rather than a tree, none of arm 14 having been applied, so it reads the proposed dictionaries out of section 21.14 of the plan by brace matching and the live ones out of the two configuration files the same way, which means a snippet edited in the plan is checked on the next run rather than silently diverging from what was checked once. Fifty four assertions pass and none fails. Two of them had to be corrected before they did, and both corrections are recorded in that section, the sagittal closure identity needing the right leg's sign convention because the two hip pitch axes are anti-parallel, and the audit of hip yaw bearing lines needing attribution to the enclosing construct rather than a count, since a line may carry the name twice and a comment carries it harmlessly.
