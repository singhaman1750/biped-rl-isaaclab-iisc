# Plans Index

This directory holds the prescriptive documents of the simulation repository, the designs and implementation plans that propose changes to the code within it. A plan document states a problem, grounds it in evidence, surveys whatever literature bears on it, and then sets out the concrete edits that realise the remedy, ordinarily at the granularity of a named file and a named symbol. Documents that record established facts rather than proposed changes live in [../context](../context/README.md) instead.

### co_optimisation_cleanup.md

The specification and record of the observation and network configuration cleanup that unifies the co-optimisation training path around four named observation sets, `policy`, `critic`, `encoderIn` and `gtEncoderOut`, so that changing what the estimator reads or predicts is a change to a runner configuration's `obs_groups` mapping alone, rather than an edit to three files and, in the general case, a new module class and a new algorithm class beside it. It establishes by direct inspection that `resolve_obs_groups` rejects a synthetic name declared in the configuration, so the estimator output is instead appended to the module's own copy of `obs_groups["policy"]` and injected into a shallow copy of the observation before each forward pass, presented to the inherited `ActorCritic` machinery as one further actor observation so that its width, its empirical normalisation and its concatenation are all handled without arithmetic of the module's own. Four experiment configurations follow from the design directly, differing only in what `encoderIn` and `gtEncoderOut` name, and the plain TRON1 SoleFoot task, the HIMLoco tasks and the quadruped tasks are each discharged in turn as work this document's edits must not disturb.

Its outcome section records that the work landed across fourteen source files of this repository and one of the workspace, `/ws/djinn`, on 2026-09-10, and again, in full, on 2026-09-17, the first implementation having existed only as uncommitted working tree state and having been destroyed without trace by the incident `../../plans/repo_restoration.md` documents. The four divergences the first implementation's validation surfaced, the quadruped estimator's hidden dimension count, the quadruped history group's flattening, `CoptEstimator`'s docstring, and the dead `torch.nn` and `numpy` imports left behind by the encoder decoder deletion, were reapplied on the second implementation from this document's own section 11 rather than rediscovered, since the record of what to correct had itself survived, being an entry in this document rather than in the tree the incident reached.

### co_optimisation_audit_implementation.md

The executable form of the co-optimisation audit, and the document to work from when applying it. It continues the audit's investigation at the point of writing code, and its section 1 records what the code contradicted. The most consequential is that the Isaac Sim MuJoCo importer authors a revolute joint's `localRot0` as the child body's frame composed with the joint's axis alignment, so the audit's in place rotation write would have turned every knee about the wrong axis, and the plan composes the two. The biped's foot target and standing height are taken from the exact sagittal chain of the emitted document rather than from fitted constants, which were 11 mm off and blind to a categorical pick of the ankle housing. The first draft gave the copt quadruped its own asset configuration and frame scheme document because the configuration the audit would have edited is shared by six plain and HIM tasks, which the revision of 2026-10-06 reversed.

Its method is worth noting. Every edit was applied to a sandbox copy of the working tree, placed ahead of the installed packages in the training container, so the diffs it carries are the ones that were tested and nothing in the tree or the container's site packages was touched. Its section 9 records the results, among them that every swap of short training runs of both robots measured all 256 environments within 0.006 mm of their solved poses, that a design written in place through one environment stands correctly in another that was never written, settling the audit's N1, and that the 61 non co-optimisation tasks' configurations are unchanged field for field. Its section 9.8 is the validation run to repeat on the real tree before any long run.

The revision of 2026-10-06 answered the plan's review. Its section 4.1 shows, from the importer's code and the committed assets, that the quadruped's fold lived in its joint nominals while the biped's lived in its bodies, argues from the Isaac Lab code that reads the nominals why a searched fold must live in the bodies, and finds by a term by term search and a simulator comparison that moving the quadruped's fold is a change of joint coordinates alone, the critic's `robot_joint_pos` being the one term that reports a nominal itself. Its section 9.4 records every generator of `usd_generator.py` training on both robots with every environment standing within 0.006 mm of its design's solved pose at every swap.

The revision of 2026-10-07 answered a second review. Its section 3.1 finds that the categorical search was kept off the in place route by the editor's search for a box alone, every quantity an actuator pick moves being a stage attribute, a cylinder's radius and height, a housing's mass and inertia and a joint's `localPos0`, and its section 9.2 measures a categorical design written in place against its own conversion, equal in every body pose, mass, inertia, centre of mass and cooked collider volume to 7.2e-7. Its section 7 records that Isaac Lab exposes no joint frame, so the nominal pose observation reads each folded body's joint frame on the stage once per initialisation of the articulation, and agrees with the fold the bodies realise to 1.2e-7 rad. Its section 1 adds that the biped's co-optimisation runner takes 25 steps per iteration, so the curriculum rescale the audit made for the quadruped applies to the biped too.

### kscale_integration.md

The implementation brief for the KScale biped, which entered the tree as a copy of the SD_BRS1 configuration taken at an earlier point in its development and has since fallen behind it on every axis the gait work stream advanced. It compares the two robots morphologically, by reward term, by agent configuration and by environment configuration, closing that comparison with a table of every difference and its consequence. It then reports the measurement of the KScale URDF and its collision meshes, which is the material the original port lacked, and specifies the robot agnostic script that performs it. Its proposed changes rest on three findings that the configuration file cannot show, that the root link frame is rotated ninety degrees from the Isaac Lab convention so that the commanded forward velocity is lateral, that the foot link frame carries its vertical on y so that the sole sits 0.043 m below the ankle rather than the 0.19 m a reader assuming the SD_BRS1 convention obtains, and that the launcher has no branch for this robot and fails open, silently training the TRON1 SoleFoot instead. Its most useful negative result is that no reward function in the shared package hard codes a name, a count or a geometry, so the four missing reward terms are restored by configuration alone and no shared code is edited.

Its chapter 5 opens a second phase and is the part a reader arriving after the first training runs should read first. Parity with the SD_BRS1 was the right criterion for the port and it is exhausted, because a ported reward term is silent about every degree of freedom the exemplar mechanism does not possess, and the two robots differ in kind at exactly one place, the hip yaw joint being `type="fixed"` on the SD_BRS1 and revolute over plus and minus ninety degrees on the KScale. The chapter measures the consequence in the run of 2026-08-25, where the off axis hip joints run at 4.2 times the SD_BRS1's per joint excursion and where the excursion correlates with yaw tracking at a partial correlation of 0.927 once linear tracking is controlled for, which is the signature of a policy turning by twisting its legs rather than by placing its feet. It surveys the three families of published treatment, establishes by forward kinematics that a KScale foot's heading is 0.995 times its hip yaw angle and nothing else, and proposes one swing gated and dead banded task space reward with its weight budgeted against the run's own logged term magnitudes. Its principal negative result is that the `feet_yaw_alignment` function already sitting unwired in the shared module must not simply be wired, its Euler yaw extraction being correct for the SD_BRS1 foot frame and wrong by 70 to 110 degrees on the KScale's permuted one, such that at any negative weight it would drive the feet towards the very splay it is meant to remove.

The first training run against chapter 5 has since been measured, and the appended material dated 2026-08-28 is where a reader should now begin, because it revises the chapter's central assumption rather than extending it. The term worked as an instrument of measurement, the differential mode carrying roughly 78 per cent of the penalty exactly as the decomposition predicted, and the off axis excursion fell 37 per cent, but the yaw tracking fell 47 per cent and the heading penalty was never driven low during sustained locomotion at any point in twenty six thousand iterations. The reason is quantified rather than conjectured, the KScale sole having an aspect ratio of 2.481 so that a splayed foot presents a 1.874 fold greater lateral extent, and single support occupying 0.76 of the commanded gait cycle, so the splay buys a stability margin against which a penalty costing 4.80 per cent of return cannot compete at any weight the tracking terms would survive. The revision accordingly proposes a hard constraint expressed as a termination in place of a larger price, together with a swing gated hip yaw reward whose purpose is to force the foot to land straight so that the existing slide and landing penalties may tax its rotation under load, and it records that the sign of the splay, inward or outward, is still not established by any measurement taken so far.

Section 5.1.2 was rewritten on 2026-09-03 and is now the section to read, the earlier passes having accumulated to 680 lines of which the derivations belong to sections 3, 5.1.1 and 7 and are not repeated. It carries the state of the implementation, a table of the five runs with the rationale and outcome of each, the measurements those runs produced, the configuration proposed as Baseline 4 and a single ablation sequence. The finding it turns on is that Baseline 3 achieved every objective set for it, cutting the pigeon toed splay bias by 70 per cent and honouring the stance width floor for the first time, and paid by ceasing to walk, and that the cause is a ratio the two group description of the preceding run concealed. Aggregated by group, the gait block was not demoted against the task block at all, and was demoted 2.71 fold against the style block, 1.94 fold against the regularisation block and 28.7 fold against the flat survival bonus, each of which is cheaper to satisfy when the robot stays on the ground. Two mechanical defects are recorded alongside it, an ankle roll standing at a stop for 80 per cent of steps and a hip yaw at a tenth of every other joint's stiffness, and new identified gains addressing both are now in the tree, so the sequence opens with a gain only control before any reward change is attempted.

### kscale_actuator_limits_fix.md

The diagnosis of a failure that runs opposite to intuition, a biped ceasing to walk when its actuators are made stronger and faster. Its evidential base is unusually clean, three runs sharing one reward set, one gain set and one curriculum and differing only in nine actuator ceilings, with the middle run isolating a single ceiling at a single joint group.

Its central finding is that the effort ceiling was never a safety margin on this robot and was instead its control surface. The policy commands an unbounded position target through an action term whose `clip` is unset, so the hip pitch stands on its torque envelope for 97.7 per cent of steps and the two ankles for some seventy, and the stiffness of those joints is not the operative parameter for most of an episode. From that the root cause follows. A hip pitch limited to 60 Nm cannot hold the machine in a crouch deeper than about 0.62 m, so the deep crouch is mechanically unreachable and the only way for a policy to survive an episode is to step. At the published 84 Nm the crouch is holdable, it collects the survival bonus and avoids the only penalised termination, and the policy finds it at iteration 1500 where the run that eventually walked was still falling in every episode at iteration 3000.

Two secondary results are recorded because neither is visible in a configuration file. The `DCMotor` torque speed envelope contributes an effective viscous damping equal to `saturation_effort / velocity_limit` whenever a joint is saturated, and at the ankles of the working run that term supplied 1.70 Nm s per radian against a configured damping of 0.5 at the ankle roll, being 77 per cent of that joint's total. And the run that walks does so with its ankle roll against a mechanical stop for 53 to 56 per cent of steps and its hip roll for 46 to 49, which corroborates the addition of 2026-09-03 to `../context/KScale.md` and establishes that the working gait was never a healthy one.

Its most useful negative results are two remedies excluded before they are proposed. Reducing the action scale is excluded because this workspace has measured it to raise stop residency rather than lower it, and pricing the crouch more heavily is excluded because `pen_base_height` already stands at minus 30 and the failing run pays ninety two times more on it than the working one, which is the signature of a price being paid rather than avoided.


### kscale_gait_shaping.md

The reading of the four run ablation of 2026-09-17 and 2026-09-18, and the document a reader arriving at the pigeon toed gait should now begin with, because it reaches a conclusion the four earlier passes of [kscale_integration.md](kscale_integration.md) did not, that the splay and the crouch are one phenomenon and that pricing either one relocates it onto the other.

Its evidential method is worth noting apart from its findings. The four dumped configurations are compared exhaustively rather than by inspection, which is what establishes that the nominal pose and the actuator set are identical across the sequence and disposes of several hypotheses before they are raised. The causal claims are then drawn from within run correlations computed per environment and averaged, rather than from the four between run contrasts, because the document first establishes that no two arms differ in one variable and that the training budget differs by a factor of 3.2 between the two pairs. A reader who wants one number from it should take the correlation of plus 0.749 between run A's instantaneous base height and its instantaneous toe-in, which is the substitution measured inside one policy at the timescale of a step and is the finding the whole argument rests upon.

Three of its results bear beyond the immediate question. The lateral moment balance of its section 4.2 states in one line why this machine cannot balance on one foot without geometric help, the demand of 14.5 to 15.0 Nm standing against an 11.9 Nm ceiling with at most 8.42 Nm of relief available and that only at the edge of the sole. The action authority audit of its section 4.6 shows that a single action scale applied to joints whose ceiling to stiffness ratios span 0.119 to 0.420 rad cannot serve them, and predicts from the arithmetic alone which two joints will be found jammed, which the dumps then confirm. And its section 4.5 argues that the base height target is set at a pose the policy does not want, the knee to height curve of this robot being flat across the first half radian so that the target constrains the knee hardly at all while placing it beside its own singularity, which is corroborated by two of four runs converging independently on a knee more than twice the configured nominal and being the better gait for it.

Its ablation is ordered so that the mechanical question is settled before any reward question is asked, and it opens with a reference arm rather than with an intervention, because the working tree has already raised the ankle ceiling to 17 Nm and already carries run C's reward set. It excludes four remedies explicitly, the reduction of the global action scale, the further raising of the splay price, the enabling of the mirror loss and the further raising of the height price, and in each case it gives the measurement or the algebra that excludes it.

Its section 21, added 2026-09-23 as section 15, renumbered to 17 on the same date, to 19 on 2026-09-28 and to 21 on 2026-09-30, and PHYSICALLY REORDERED on that date so that the order of its sub-sections is the order of execution, is the section to work from when implementing rather than when choosing. It falls into two halves, sections 22.1 to 21.14 being the record of what has been executed, abandoned or superseded together with four dated records of the working tree, and sections 21.1 to 21.28 being the arms that have NOT been applied in the order they should be run, each heading carrying its RUN position so that a reader deciding what to do next needs only that half. Every arm stands there as a sub-section of its own, carrying six elements in a fixed order, being the status, the rationale, the code as the tree holds it and as the arm would have it, the design decisions with the alternatives they reject, the parameterisation with the arithmetic behind each number, and the prediction with the observation that would falsify it. Three properties of the action term's dictionary forms are worth carrying away from it without reading it. A dictionary valued `scale` initialises to ONES so an omitted joint silently takes 1.0, while a dictionary valued `clip` initialises to plus and minus infinity so an omitted joint is merely unclipped, which makes omission catastrophic in the first and harmless in the second. The hip roll and the hip pitch carry mirrored ranges between the two legs, so a clip may not use the `(left|right)` pattern that the scale may. And the clip must be set outside the soft position limits rather than at them, or `pen_joint_pos_limits` is silenced and the arm loses the measurement it exists to take.

Sections 10 through 14 were added on 2026-09-22 after the first two arms of that ablation were run, and they change what a reader should take from the document. The ablation's own premise, that the ankle is short of torque, is refuted by the arms it proposed, the ankle roll remaining latched against its mechanical stop at every ceiling from 11.9 to 34 Nm while the splay and the fall rate both rise with the ceiling. The fault is relocated from the actuator to the action space, the policy commanding a target more than twice the joint's travel and the configuration carrying nothing that prices a target the joint cannot reach, and the remedy accordingly moves from raising a ceiling to bounding a command. A reader who wants one number from the extension should take the reconstructed commanded target of 0.58 radians against a mechanical stop of 0.2618, whose excursion beyond the stop triples as the ceiling triples, because it is the measurement that distinguishes a joint driven into its limit by contact from a joint held there on purpose.

Two results of the extension generalise past this robot. An exponential reward kernel whose width is smaller than the deviation it must correct supplies no gradient rather than a weak one, so raising its weight cannot rescue it, and the document measures the three in air terms of this configuration sitting on both sides of that threshold with the one inside it being the only one that acted. And a DC motor whose saturation effort equals its effort ceiling has no constant torque region at all, its corner velocity being zero, so two configurations that differ in their ceiling may deliver identical torque across the whole of the working speed range, which is what happened here between the 11.9 and the 17 Nm arms above 11.31 rad/s.

## What belongs here

A plan belongs in this directory when its every edit falls inside this repository and it requires no coordinated change to the workspace, the vendored libraries, or the container tooling. A plan for a new robot asset and its task registration, a plan to restructure the environment configuration layering, or a plan to add a reward family would all qualify. A plan that touches the training entry point together with the co-optimisation package and the `djinn` command surface would not, and belongs at the workspace level.

Where the distinction is genuinely unclear, prefer the workspace level, since a plan is more useful over-scoped and findable than correctly scoped and overlooked.

## Conventions

A plan names its target as a file path and a symbol, and quotes surrounding code to locate an edit. Those quotations drift as the sources change, so an edit should be located by symbol name and the quoted code treated as corroboration rather than as an address.

Each plan carries a status banner immediately beneath its title, stating whether its proposals are implemented, partially implemented, superseded, or outstanding, and the date that status was last verified against the live sources. The register above must record the same status, so that a reader may triage without opening the document. On completing a plan, update its banner and record the outcome, together with any divergence from the plan, in the corresponding context document, so that the plan remains a statement of intent and the context record remains the statement of fact.
